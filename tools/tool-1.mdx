Text manipulation is one of those things that comes up constantly — in coding, writing, data wrangling, and everywhere in between. You paste in a blob of text and need it trimmed, sorted, or in a different case before you can use it somewhere else. The Text Processor was born from that daily friction: instead of opening a code editor to write a one-liner or hunting for a trustworthy website, you paste your text here and get the result immediately.

## What It Does

The tool supports a focused set of operations that cover most real-world text tasks:

- **Trim whitespace** — strip leading, trailing, or excess internal spaces and tabs
- **Convert case** — switch between UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case, and camelCase
- **Remove duplicate lines** — deduplicate a list while preserving the order of first appearances
- **Sort lines alphabetically** — reorder lines A → Z or Z → A, with optional case-insensitive sorting
- **Escape / unescape special characters** — convert characters like `<`, `>`, `&`, and quotes to HTML entities and back
- **Count words and characters** — get a quick summary of the text's length without pasting it into a word processor

## How to Use

<Steps>
  <Step title="Paste or type your text">
    Click into the input box and paste the text you want to process. You can also type directly — the tool works on anything from a single word to thousands of lines.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select an operation">
    Choose the transformation you want from the operation menu. Each option shows a short description so you know exactly what it will do before you apply it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the output">
    The result appears instantly in the output area. Click the copy button to grab it to your clipboard, ready to paste wherever you need it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Example

Here's what the **Convert to Title Case** operation does to a few lines of lowercase text:

<CodeGroup>
  ```plaintext Input
  the quick brown fox
  jumps over the lazy dog
  a journey of a thousand miles
  ```

  ```plaintext Output
  The Quick Brown Fox
  Jumps Over The Lazy Dog
  A Journey Of A Thousand Miles
  ```
</CodeGroup>

<Tip>
  You can chain multiple operations by applying them one after another. For example, first trim whitespace to clean up messy input, then convert to title case, then remove any duplicate lines — each step feeds into the next.
</Tip>

## Technical Notes

The Text Processor is built with vanilla JavaScript and runs entirely inside your browser tab. No text you enter is ever sent to a server — all transformations happen locally on your device. Because there's no network dependency, the tool continues to work even when you're offline, as long as the page has already loaded.
